Guideline points checked November 2026:

  • ATI Nursing: Evidence-Based Practice exemplars (Introduction to Best Practices; Individual Preferences).
  • NPIAP, EPUAP, PPPIA. International Pressure Injury Guideline, 4th edition, Repositioning chapter. (Individualized schedules; factors including sleep and comfort; 2- or 3-hour intervals on a suitable surface; not routinely extending to 4–6 hours; 30° lateral positioning; involving the person)
  • NPIAP, EPUAP, PPPIA. Prevention and Treatment of Pressure Ulcers/Injuries: Clinical Practice Guideline, 2019, nutrition recommendations as summarized by the Association of Nutrition & Foodservice Professionals (2021). (Nutrition screening, dietitian referral, honoring cultural and religious food preferences)
  • Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality. Preventing Pressure Injuries in Hospitals, Module 1: Understanding Why Change Is Needed. (2.5 million patients, about 60,000 deaths, up to $11 billion a year, most preventable; estimates citing AHRQ 2013)
  • Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services. Hospital-Acquired Conditions. (Stage III and IV pressure ulcers; no additional payment when not present on admission)
  • National Quality Forum. Serious Reportable Events. (Stage 3, 4, or unstageable pressure injury acquired after admission)
  • Pressure injuries in the pediatric population: many questions, few answers. Wound Management & Prevention (HMP Global). (50%–90% of pediatric pressure injuries device-related; neonatal skin; Braden QD)
  • Hartford Institute for Geriatric Nursing. Evidence-Based Geriatric Nursing Protocols for Best Practice, Pressure Injury Prevention (updated January 2025). (Do not massage bony prominences; avoid donut-shaped devices)
  • National Institute for Health and Care Excellence. Pressure ulcers: prevention and management (CG179). (Do not offer skin massage or rubbing to prevent pressure ulcers)
  • Bergstrom N, Braden BJ, et al. The Braden Scale for Predicting Pressure Sore Risk. Nursing Research, 1987. (Subscales and 6–23 range)
