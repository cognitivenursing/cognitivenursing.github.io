Scope & Delegation · Scoring

Multi-Patient Delegation Case Rubric

Shared ADN and PN rubric. Both versions are scored on the same five criteria.

Expected level of achievement

80% of students will achieve 3 (Competent) or higher on all five rubric criteria. One criterion scored 2 counts the student as not meeting the expectation. Report ADN and PN results separately.

Rubric

Scoring: 5 Exemplary, 4 Proficient, 3 Competent, 2 Developing, 1 Beginning. A missing response scores 1.

1. Recognizes and analyzes chart cues
Recognize cues · Analyze cues		Identifies every stability-relevant finding for all six patients from the EHR (flowsheet trends, MAR, labs, notes) and explains what each finding means for delegation.Identifies the key finding and supporting cues for each patient, and explains what the findings mean for delegation for most patients.Identifies the key finding for each patient and correctly calls each patient stable or unstable/unpredictable.Misses or misreads one patient's key finding, or names findings without linking them to stability.Misses key findings for two or more patients, or judges stability without chart data.
2. Matches tasks to legal scope
NC NPA · 21 NCAC 36 · NA I/II task lists		Places every task within the correct role, cites the rule or task list that governs each one, and identifies where 4 East policy is stricter than the law.Places every task within the correct role and cites the governing rule or task list for each task at the licensed/unlicensed or NA I/NA II line.Places every task within legal scope. At most one minor citation gap; no out-of-scope assignment.One task placed outside legal scope for a role, with no harm to a patient in the case.Two or more out-of-scope placements, or any out-of-scope placement involving assessment, evaluation, or an unstable patient.
3. Justifies assignment and delegation decisions
Prioritize hypotheses · Generate solutions		Applies all five rights to each decision, separates assignment from delegation, and addresses agency policy and the individual's validated competence.Applies all five rights to each decision, but does not consistently separate assignment from delegation or address both agency policy and individual competence.Gives a correct, defensible decision for every patient, justified with at least the right task, circumstance, and person.Decisions mostly correct, but justification is generic (“stable patient”) or omits the right circumstance or right person.Decisions are unjustified, or one or more would place a patient at risk.
4. Revises the plan when conditions change
Take action		Responds to every Phase 2 change with a revised plan: re-prioritizes, reallocates every remaining task, and states who goes to the deteriorating patient and why.Responds correctly to every Phase 2 change and states who goes to the deteriorating patient and why; reallocation of remaining tasks is partial or implied.Responds correctly to each Phase 2 change, including the deteriorating patient and the staffing loss.Responds to the deteriorating patient but leaves another Phase 2 change unaddressed or handled outside scope.Delegates care of the deteriorating patient, or does not revise the plan.
5. Directs, supervises, and evaluates
Evaluate outcomes		Gives each delegatee specific directions (what, when, what to report, report parameters), states follow-up timing, and writes a complete SBAR with a specific recommendation.Gives directions with report parameters and follow-up timing for each delegated task, and writes a complete SBAR; the recommendation is general.Gives directions with report parameters for each delegated task, describes follow-up, and writes an SBAR with all four parts.Directions lack report parameters or follow-up, or the SBAR is missing a part.No directions, no follow-up, or no SBAR.

What “Competent” means in each role

CriterionADN (RN team leader)PN (LPN on RN-led team)
1. CuesInterprets findings and decides stability.Collects data, recognizes the relationship between data and the patient's status, and identifies the need for immediate intervention (21 NCAC 36 .0225(b)).
2. ScopeAssigns within LPN scope; delegates within NA I / NA II lists.Accepts only what the LPN can safely perform; recognizes what must go to the RN; delegates within NA I / NA II lists.
3. DecisionsMakes the assignment and delegation decisions.Decides whether to accept the assignment and delegates within it. A request to clarify or decline an assignment counts as a decision.
4. RevisionRe-plans the team's work and retains the deteriorating patient.Takes immediate action within LPN scope and reports to the RN in time for the patient's needs.
5. SupervisionSupervises, evaluates outcomes, and revises the plan of care.Supervision is limited to validating that delegated tasks were done as directed and to standard (.0225(d)(2)(E)). The LPN proposes plan changes to the RN rather than making them.
