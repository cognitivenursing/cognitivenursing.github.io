How you are scored

Faculty score your submission on five rubric criteria: (1) recognizing and analyzing chart cues, (2) matching tasks to legal scope, (3) justifying decisions with the five rights, (4) revising the plan when conditions change, and (5) directing, supervising, and evaluating delegated work. You meet the expectation with a 3 (Competent) or higher on every criterion.

Work through four filters, in order

For each task, ask: (1) Law. Does the NC Nursing Practice Act or the NCBON task list allow this role to do it? (2) Agency policy. Does 4 East allow it? Policy can be stricter than the law. (3) The person. Is this individual validated and current on this task? (4) The patient. Is this patient stable and predictable right now? A task can pass the first three and still fail the fourth.

    4 East unit policy excerpts

    Agency policy for this case. Your clinical site’s policies may differ.

    Medication administration
    • IV push medications on 4 East are administered by an RN.
    • LPNs who are unit-validated may give PO, SC, IM, and IV piggyback medications through an established peripheral IV.
    • Unlicensed personnel (NA I, NA II) do not administer medications.
    Unlicensed personnel
    • An NA I performs tasks on the NCBON Nurse Aide I task list. An NA II may also perform NA II tasks. Either performs a task only when a licensed nurse delegates it for a specific patient.
    • NCBON classifies fingerstick blood glucose as a non-nursing function. On 4 East an NA I or NA II with the unit competency may do it.
    • Assessment, planning, evaluation, initial teaching, and nursing judgment are not delegated.
    Hypoglycemia protocol (adult)
    • Blood glucose <70 mg/dL, patient alert and able to swallow: give 15 g fast-acting carbohydrate (4 oz juice). Recheck in 15 minutes and repeat if still <70.
    • Unable to swallow or NPO: glucagon 1 mg IM or dextrose 50% 25 mL IV per order set.
    • Notify the provider for any value <70 mg/dL. Hold scheduled prandial insulin until the provider is notified.
    Oxygen protocol

    A nurse may start or titrate oxygen by nasal cannula up to 4 L/min to keep SpO₂ ≥92%, then notifies the provider.

    Rapid response criteria

    Any staff member may call the rapid response team for: HR <40 or >130; SBP <90; RR <8 or >28; SpO₂ <90% despite oxygen; new chest pain unrelieved by protocol; acute change in mental status; or any staff member worried about the patient.
